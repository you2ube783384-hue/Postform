# 1. PROJECT MISSION
Build a production-ready, responsive e-commerce website for POSTFORM, a fashion/streetwear resale business that acquires available clothing, footwear and accessories and resells them internationally.
The primary objective is NOT to build a complicated enterprise commerce platform. The objective is to create a visually exceptional, memorable and highly usable storefront with a distinctive Neo-Brutalist / Editorial Fashion aesthetic, while keeping the business workflow intentionally simple.
The website must feel like a real, premium streetwear brand—not a generic AI-generated template.
The current visual reference is a product-page design with warm white background, strong black rectangular borders, bold typography, monospace/technical UI text, yellow primary actions, purple secondary accents, structured product imagery and a highly organized editorial layout. Use this reference as a design-language reference only. Do not copy its branding, exact layout, assets, text, or identity.

# 2. BRAND PROFILE
Brand name: POSTFORM
Logo: There is currently no graphic logo. Use the word “POSTFORM” as a black typographic wordmark. Do not invent a complex icon unless it genuinely improves the identity. The wordmark should feel intentional and distinctive through typography, spacing and proportion.
Business model: POSTFORM is a reseller/curated-stock business. Inventory may vary depending on what products the business obtains. Products can include new, used, vintage, or other available stock, so the product model must support varying conditions and inventory.
Main categories:
• T-shirts
• Oversized T-shirts
• Shirts
• Hoodies
• Pants
• Jeans
• Sneakers
• Accessories
• Additional categories as inventory evolves
Primary audience:
Men approximately 18–30, streetwear and casual-fashion customers, college students, sneaker culture, trend-conscious shoppers, plus an undefined broader international audience.
Brand personality:
• Dark / edgy
• Minimal / premium
• Streetwear-oriented
• Confident
• Editorial rather than corporate
• Functional rather than decorative

# 3. CORE DESIGN DIRECTION
Design the site as a refined Neo-Brutalist + Fashion Editorial commerce experience.
Brutalism intensity: approximately 4–5/10. It should have personality and strong visual structure without becoming experimental to the point of harming usability.
Important: Do NOT simply recreate the supplied reference screenshot. Evolve its visual language into a more polished POSTFORM identity.
Design principles:
• Warm milky white background rather than harsh pure white.
• Strong black borders are a defining visual feature.
• Use rectangular/structured components.
• Corners should be sharp or use only very small radius values.
• High contrast and strong hierarchy.
• Large, confident typography.
• Combine modern fashion/editorial typography with monospace/technical UI typography.
• Use black as the dominant structural color.
• Use yellow and purple as controlled accent colors.
• Product photography should be visually dominant.
• Use generous whitespace while maintaining a dense, intentional grid.
• Avoid generic SaaS aesthetics.
• Avoid rounded “app card” design.
• Avoid excessive shadows.
• Avoid gradients.
• Avoid glassmorphism.
• Avoid excessive animation.
• Avoid unnecessary decorative effects.
The site should communicate: “This is a cool, curated streetwear store that looks expensive and different.”

# 4. COLOR SYSTEM
Establish a reusable design-token system.
Base:
• Background: warm milky white / off-white.
• Primary text and structural borders: near-black/black.
• Secondary text: muted dark gray.
• Accent 1: energetic yellow, primarily for important CTAs and highlights.
• Accent 2: controlled purple, primarily for links, secondary states and small highlights.
Do not flood the interface with yellow or purple. Accents should guide attention.
All colors must be centralized as design tokens/CSS variables so the brand can be adjusted later without rewriting components.

# 5. TYPOGRAPHY
Use two complementary typography roles:
1. Editorial/display typography:
• Strong, bold, fashion-oriented.
• Used for hero headings, major section headings and important product names.
• Should feel contemporary and premium.
2. Technical/utility typography:
• Monospace or technical-looking font.
• Used for navigation details, metadata, labels, prices where appropriate, buttons, stock information, order information and small UI elements.
Do not use excessive font families. Prefer a compact, intentional typography system with a clear hierarchy.
Typography must remain highly readable on mobile.

# 6. GLOBAL UI LANGUAGE
Use a strong visual system across the entire application:
• Black 2–3px-ish borders where appropriate, with a consistent token.
• Hard/controlled rectangular geometry.
• Clear separation between content zones.
• Minimal border radius.
• Strong hover/focus states.
• Buttons should feel physical and tactile.
• Primary CTA buttons may use yellow with black text and a strong black border.
• Secondary buttons may be white/off-white with black border.
• Purple may be used for links, small states or selected/active UI.
• Avoid “floating glass” UI.
• Avoid excessive drop shadows.
• Use subtle depth through borders, offsets or controlled transforms instead of modern soft UI.
Micro-interactions should be purposeful:
• Buttons can shift 1–3px on press.
• Product cards can make a subtle image movement on hover.
• Links can use small underline/offset effects.
• Cart quantity changes should feel immediate.
• No long transitions.
• No scroll-jacking.
• No excessive parallax.
• Respect prefers-reduced-motion.

# 27. ADMIN AUTHENTICATION
The admin area must not be publicly editable.

# 28. IMAGE STORAGE STRATEGY
The desired business workflow is to keep product images close to the project rather than relying entirely on a database.
Important deployment constraint: a Vercel deployment filesystem should not be treated as a persistent writable disk.
Therefore, implement one of these practical approaches:
Preferred approach:
• Git-backed product assets/content where admin changes can commit product data/images to the repository, if the chosen deployment workflow supports it.
Fallback:
• Allow external image URLs as a supported image source.
If an image-upload workflow requires persistent object storage, use an appropriate storage provider rather than pretending /public is a persistent writable database.
The admin product form should make the image source explicit and validate URLs where URL-based images are used.
Do not store large binary image blobs in a relational database.
